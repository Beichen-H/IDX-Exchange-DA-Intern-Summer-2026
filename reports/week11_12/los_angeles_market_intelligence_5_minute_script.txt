Los Angeles County Market Intelligence
Five-Minute Presentation Script

Slide 1 - Introduction (0:00-0:45)
Good afternoon. Today I will present a market intelligence review of the Los Angeles County residential market. The data covers January 2024 through June 2026. For the main comparisons, I use the latest twelve months, from July 2025 through June 2026, and compare them with the previous twelve months. I focus on five questions: what happened to prices, how quickly homes sold, how closely closing prices tracked original list prices, whether market activity expanded or contracted, and which agents and offices led the market. I also applied business rules and interquartile range screens so that extreme or invalid records would not distort the typical market story.

Slide 2 - Market overview (0:45-1:35)
The first finding is that prices remained stable while the selling process became slower. The median closing price in the latest twelve months was $865,000. That represents an increase of only 0.6 percent from the previous twelve months, so this is a mostly flat price environment. At the same time, median days on market increased from 16 days to 18 days. That is a 12.5 percent increase. Closed sales totaled 36,286, which was 1.9 percent lower. Taken together, these measures show that buyers needed more time to make decisions, even though the median price did not fall materially.

Slide 3 - Pricing trends (1:35-2:25)
The pricing measures provide more evidence of a modest shift in negotiating conditions. Median price per square foot was $587, down 1.0 percent from the previous twelve months. The median closing price was 99.7 percent of the original list price. That ratio declined by approximately 0.3 percentage points. A typical property still sold very close to its original asking price, so the data does not indicate widespread distress. However, the combination of slightly lower price per square foot, a lower sale-to-original-list ratio, and longer market time suggests that accurate initial pricing became more important. Sellers who started above market evidence faced a greater risk of waiting longer or accepting a reduction.

Slide 4 - Market activity (2:25-3:15)
Market activity also softened, but listings declined more sharply than completed sales. New listings totaled 60,213 during the latest twelve months, down 7.1 percent. Closed sales totaled 36,286, down 1.9 percent. Closed transaction volume was $35.7B, a decline of 1.3 percent. The monthly chart also shows the normal seasonal pattern, with activity strengthening during the spring. The important comparison is the difference in the annual changes. Fewer properties entered the market, while the decline in completed sales was much smaller. This helped prevent a large increase in available supply and supported the relatively stable median price.

Slide 5 - Competitive landscape (3:15-4:10)
The competitive analysis identifies the leading listing agents and offices within the cleaned Los Angeles County sample. Compass ranked first among listing offices with $3.1B in closed volume and 2,552 units. Cesi Pagano ranked first among listing agents by closed volume, with approximately $127.8M and 132 units. The remaining rankings show that both large brokerages and productive individual agents contribute meaningful volume. The Tableau competitive workbook allows the user to filter these rankings by month, city, county, ZIP code, and property subtype. Those filters are important because countywide rank can hide local strength. An office that ranks lower across the full county may lead a specific ZIP code or property segment.

Slide 6 - Key takeaways (4:10-5:00)
I will close with five conclusions. First, Los Angeles County prices were stable, with the median closing price increasing only 0.6 percent. Second, homes took longer to sell, as median days on market increased from 16 to 18 days. Third, buyers gained modest negotiating room because the sale-to-original-list ratio and price per square foot both declined slightly. Fourth, new listings fell faster than closed sales, which limited the increase in supply. Fifth, competitive performance depends on the local segment, so agent and office rankings should be reviewed by ZIP code and property subtype. For sellers, the practical implication is to price from current comparable sales. Buyers can use the slower pace to compare alternatives and negotiate carefully. Brokerages can use the dashboard to identify local markets where their unit share or sales volume has room to improve. Thank you, and I am happy to answer questions.

Methodology note
Source: CRMLS Residential listing and sold exports, January 2024 through June 2026. The analysis applies positive-value business rules, 1.5 times IQR screens for closing price, days on market, and price per square foot, and a 0.50 to 1.50 range for the close-to-original-list ratio. Findings are descriptive and do not establish causation.
